What if overdue payments chased themselves?

Payment Collect

Payment follow-ups for manufacturers. Overdue bills come straight from Tally and go out on WhatsApp with a payment link.

Talk to me about it

Why it exists

Indian MSMEs wait 73 days on average to get an invoice paid (Recordent, 2026), and ₹7.34 lakh crore sits stuck in delayed payments (GAME, FISME and C2FO, 2024). At most manufacturers I know, following up is still one person with Tally on one screen and a phone in hand.

Before (Phone and Tally): Someone checks Tally for who owes what; Calls each party, one by one; Ledgers sent by hand; The owner hears about it when cash gets tight. After (On autopilot): Overdue bills read straight from Tally; A WhatsApp reminder with the ledger and a payment link; Firmer reminders on a schedule, calls only when needed; Paid invoices mark themselves.

What’s on screen

  • Ledger and pay link in one message: The party gets their ledger PDF and a one-tap payment link on WhatsApp. No “please send the details” back and forth.
  • Firmer, on a schedule: Polite on the due date, firmer at day 7. VIP parties never get the firm one.
  • A person makes the hard call: At day 15 the party is flagged for a call, and at day 30 the owner gets an alert. Automation does the routine chasing, people handle the relationship.
  • Paid marks itself: When money comes in through the link, the invoice is marked paid. Tally catches up when the receipt is entered.

How it works

  • Sync: A small connector reads outstanding bills from Tally while it’s open. Nobody re-enters anything.
  • Remind: On the due date the party gets a WhatsApp with their ledger and a payment link.
  • Escalate: Firmer at day 7, flagged for a call at day 15, owner alerted at day 30.
  • Settle: Paying through the link marks the invoice paid on the dashboard.

Decisions, and why

  • Tally stays: Manufacturers here run on Tally, and asking them to switch kills the deal. Payment Collect reads Tally, and Tally stays the source of truth.
  • WhatsApp, not email: Industry benchmarks put WhatsApp open rates near 98%, against roughly 21% for email. A reminder nobody opens isn’t a reminder.
  • Meta’s API directly, no reseller: WhatsApp resellers add a markup on every message. Going direct keeps each reminder at a few paise.
  • A payment link in every message: “Please pay” with no easy way to pay is just noise. The link also confirms the payment without anyone checking the bank.
  • A person makes the day-15 call: In Indian B2B the relationship is the collateral. A bot calling a ten-year customer about money does more damage than the delay.
  • VIP parties skip the firm reminder: Some customers you never send a stern message to. The owner marks them once.
